A research project

Measuring how AI governance frameworks are built

Most comparisons of international AI governance ask what a framework says. This one asks how it is built: who holds the authority, how hard it is to change once agreed, and what it costs to put into practice.

Featured at GigaNet and the Internet Governance Forum 2026

This work was selected for presentation at two leading forums in internet and digital governance. Our paper, “Beyond the Veto: International AI Governance Without Power Concentration,” was accepted through anonymous review to the GigaNet Annual Symposium, the annual conference of the Global Internet Governance Academic Network. The project was also featured at the United Nations Internet Governance Forum (IGF) 2026 in Nairobi, Kenya, in Lightning Talk #40, “Discussing Global AI Governance and Power Concentration.”

What the index does

The AI Governance Design Index scores international AI governance frameworks on three structural axes — power concentration, reversibility and efficiency — each built from four equally weighted features with published scoring anchors.

The scores are descriptive, not evaluative. A framework that concentrates authority is not thereby better or worse than one that does not; the point is to make the design trade-offs visible and comparable, so that arguments about which trade-offs are worth making can start from the same picture.

  • Parveen Sheikh

    She/Her · Founder, NISA · Gender Equity, Livelihoods & Social Inclusion · Community Empowerment · Intersectional Feminist · Cinephile · Vegetarian

    Parveen is an interdisciplinary researcher working at the intersection of institutional design, power concentration, and AI governance. Her work interrogates how international bodies allocate authority, focusing specifically on verification and monitoring regimes, and how middle powers navigate frontier AI governance under great-power competition. Drawing on her academic training in social exclusion and inclusive policy, she uses applied critical theory and mixed methods to unpack how political power is exercised and institutionalized. Her research, including her work on the AI Governance Design Index and historical arms-control analogies, is grounded in a central question: how do we design frameworks that genuinely distribute power, giving the Global South substantive agency without sacrificing the ability to act decisively? She brings this practical lens from her experience founding NISA, a nonprofit in India, and drafting policy briefs inside the Indian Parliament. www.parveensheikh.com
