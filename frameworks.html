Frameworks included

Version 1.0 covers five instruments and instrument families, chosen to span the range from voluntary principles to binding regional law.

  • OECD AI Principles

    Adopted 2019 · revised 2024 · OECD/LEGAL/0449 · Recommendation, non-binding

    The first intergovernmental standard on AI, adopted by OECD members and adhered to by a wider set of countries. Its influence runs through adoption elsewhere rather than through enforcement.

    Legal instrument OECD.AI overview

  • EU Artificial Intelligence Act

    Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 · in force 1 August 2024 · binding, staged application

    The first comprehensive binding AI law, applying risk-tiered obligations across the single market with penalties for non-compliance and a central AI Office.

    Official Journal text Annotated reference

  • United Nations instruments

    Family of instruments · 2021–2024 · non-binding

    Scored as a regime rather than a single text: the UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI (2021), the Global Digital Compact adopted within the Pact for the Future (2024), and the General Assembly resolution on safe, secure and trustworthy AI (2024). See the note on bundled rows.

    UNESCO Recommendation Global Digital Compact A/RES/78/265

  • African Union instruments

    Family of instruments · 2014–2024 · mixed bindingness

    Scored as a regime: the Continental AI Strategy endorsed in 2024 (non-binding) together with the Malabo Convention on Cyber Security and Personal Data Protection, which is a binding treaty and entered into force in 2023.

    Continental AI Strategy Malabo Convention

  • ASEAN Guide on AI Governance and Ethics

    Adopted 2024 · expanded for generative AI 2025 · voluntary guidance

    Regional guidance built on national-level implementation and consensus, with no central body, no obligations and no enforcement — the clearest contrast in the set to the EU approach.

    2024 Guide (PDF) Expanded guide, generative AI (PDF)

On the figures

The figures are generated from the same numbers as the table above, so they cannot disagree with it. If you change a score, regenerate the figures as well — or the two will drift apart, which is the most common way an index like this loses a reader's trust.
